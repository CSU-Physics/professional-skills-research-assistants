Research professionalism

Keep honest records, handle project material as your supervisor directs, share credit fairly.

Professional Skills for Research Assistants · SemiAcademy

Module 4

What you will be able to do

  • Identify good recordkeeping: dated entries, raw data preserved, and corrections documented.
  • Recognize responsible handling of project data, confidential material, and AI and cloud tools under CSU policies and your supervisor’s directions.
  • Recognize that credit is based on contribution and should be discussed early.
  • Choose the right route for an error, a question or a concern.

Decision 1 of 4

Keep dated records and protect the raw data

  • A dated notebook or log: what you did, settings, file names, what you saw.
  • Keep raw data exactly as it came from the instrument. Fix processing or calibration errors in a new, documented copy.
  • Never silently overwrite. Add a dated correction that says what changed and why. If you suspect a setting or measurement was wrong, tell your supervisor promptly and investigate first. A wrong setting during a run cannot be edited away, and the run may need repeating.
  • Do not drop a result because it is inconvenient. Any exclusion needs a stated reason and your supervisor’s agreement.

Decision 2 of 4

Handle confidential material and shared data as your supervisor directs

  • Store files only where your supervisor has approved. Use approved tools and channels.
  • Share project data outside the project only with your supervisor’s permission.
  • Ask before putting unpublished data, code or project email into an AI tool or personal cloud service.
  • Verify what an AI tool produces, and say you used it where required.

Decision 3 of 4

Give and expect credit based on contribution

  • Authorship and acknowledgement follow contribution.
  • Talk about credit early, and again if the work changes.
  • If you think your contribution is missed, ask your supervisor privately and specifically.

Decision 4 of 4

Know where a concern goes

  • Routine error, your mistake or a question: your project supervisor, at once.
  • Suspected misconduct, even by your supervisor: the Research Integrity Officer (CSU Procedure 311.1). No permission needed. Ask informally first if unsure.
  • Authorship or credit dispute: your supervisor, then your department or the Office of Sponsored Programs and Research.
  • Harassment or discrimination: Human Resources, or the Title IX office.
  • Emergency: call 911 or Campus Police and follow the safety procedure, then tell your supervisor.

Worked scenario

Maya Brown's situation

Maya Brown finds the thickness values in run 12 are about 20% higher than in runs 10, 11 and 13. She thinks she may have entered a wrong setting in the analysis software. She is tempted to delete the run and save the file again under the same name.

Before the next slide, decide what you would do, and why.

Worked scenario

Weaker and stronger

Weaker

She overwrites the file and says nothing. If she was right, nobody can check it. If she was wrong, the real result is gone.

Stronger

She keeps the raw file, writes a dated log entry about what she saw and suspects, and tells Dr. Lee the same day. She checks the settings before changing anything. If the analysis setting was wrong and Dr. Lee agrees, she reprocesses the raw file and saves a new, corrected version with a note that says what changed. If the instrument had been set wrongly during the run, editing the numbers could not repair it, and Dr. Lee would decide whether to repeat the run. The weekly report then summarizes what happened.

Worked scenario

Why the stronger version works

The record now shows what happened, whichever explanation turns out to be right. If the run is excluded in the end, the reason is written down and Dr. Lee has agreed. This course gives general guidance and does not say who owns what.

Remember

  • Credit follows contribution.
  • Supervisor for errors, the Research Integrity Officer for misconduct, other offices for other concerns.
