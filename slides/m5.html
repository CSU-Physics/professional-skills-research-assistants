Time, tasks and project management

Plan a week that fits your scheduled hours, say early when a deadline is at risk, and submit your timesheet on time.

Professional Skills for Research Assistants · SemiAcademy

Module 5

What you will be able to do

  • Break a task into steps, estimate the time for each, and add a buffer.
  • Choose a realistic plan for a week around classes and scheduled hours.
  • Choose the right escalation when a deadline is at risk.
  • Identify the steps to record your hours as you work them and submit your timesheet by the payroll deadline.

Decision 1 of 4

Break a task into steps, estimate each step, add a buffer

  • Steps you could finish in one sitting.
  • Estimate hours for each. Estimates run low, so add about a quarter.
  • Put the step you understand least first.

Decision 2 of 4

Plan the week around your classes and your scheduled hours

  • Start from your scheduled hours and your class and exam calendar.
  • Fit the steps and the buffer into the hours you are scheduled to work. No planning on unagreed extra hours.
  • If an exam or class cuts into a research day, say so early and offer a revised plan.

Decision 3 of 4

Escalate early when a deadline is at risk

  • At risk means the first step ran long, not the day the work is due.
  • Say what happened, your new estimate, and what you need: time, a smaller deliverable, help or a decision.
  • Offer options. Say what you can still send by the original date.

Decision 4 of 4

Record your hours as you work them, and submit your timesheet on time

  • Record each day, from your own record. Do not rebuild a week from memory.
  • Record the hours you actually worked. Confirm in writing any change to your scheduled hours.
  • Submit by the payroll deadline your supervisor gives you. A late or missing timesheet delays your pay.
  • Send a short message with the pay period and hours. Correct anything your supervisor asks for right away.

Worked scenario

Maya Brown's situation

Maya Brown is scheduled for 9 hours: 3 each on Monday, Wednesday and Thursday. Her figure is due Friday. She plans 2 hours cleaning data, 2 calibrating, 2 on the figure and 1 on the caption: 7 hours, plus a 2-hour buffer. On Monday she uses her 3 hours on the cleaning and finds that it will take 4 hours in total, not 2.

Before the next slide, decide what you would do, and why.

Worked scenario

Weaker and stronger

Weaker

She says nothing, works extra hours without telling anyone, and sends a rushed figure on Friday. Her hours may not match her timesheet.

Stronger

On Monday she writes to Dr. Lee: what happened, the new estimate (4 hours in total, so the buffer is gone), that she can still send the calibration by Thursday, and two options for the figure deadline.

Worked scenario

Why the stronger version works

Maya escalates on the day she sees the problem, gives numbers, offers two options, and keeps her recorded hours the same as the hours she worked.

Remember
