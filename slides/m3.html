Meetings, attendance and reporting

Be on time, give notice the right way, and send a report that reads in a minute.

Professional Skills for Research Assistants · SemiAcademy

Module 3

What you will be able to do

  • Recognize respectful meeting practice in person and virtually.
  • Choose the right way to give notice of an absence or lateness.
  • Identify what a weekly report must contain and when to send it.
  • Choose how to report a problem at once and how to keep notes and action items.

Decision 1 of 4

Respect everyone’s time, in person and online

  • Arrive or join a few minutes early, prepared. Early is on time.
  • Keep to the time you are given and stop when it is up.
  • Online: check link, audio and screen sharing first, join from a quiet place, mute when not speaking.
  • Phone away. Do not work on something else.

Decision 2 of 4

Give notice of an absence or lateness the right way

  • Known conflict: ask as soon as you know, at least 24 hours ahead when you can, and say what you will send instead.
  • Illness, emergency or connection failure: tell the organizer as soon as reasonably possible and say what you will do next.
  • Late: tell the organizer before the meeting starts, by chat or text.
  • After a missed meeting, read the notes and send questions within a day or two.
  • Accommodations go through the university’s accessibility office. You decide what to share.

Decision 3 of 4

Send a weekly report that reads in a minute

  • Five parts: done, next, blocked, hours, questions.
  • Send it by the deadline your supervisor sets.
  • Behind? Send it on time anyway and say what is blocked and when the rest follows.

Decision 4 of 4

Report problems at once, and keep notes and action items

  • Equipment or data problem: tell your supervisor the same day, with facts. Leave the repair to those responsible.
  • Possible safety problem: follow the emergency and safety procedure.
  • Write down every action item: who, what, by when. Confirm in one line by email.

Worked scenario

Maya Brown's situation

Maya Brown has a chemistry exam on Tuesday, November 24, at the same time as the 10am group meeting. She learned the exam date two weeks ago.

Before the next slide, decide what you would do, and why.

Worked scenario

Weaker and stronger

Weaker

Subject “tomorrow”. “hey, cant come tmrw, got an exam. did u see my data?” Sent Monday night.

Stronger

Subject “Request to miss the Tuesday, November 24 group meeting (10am)”. “Dear Dr. Lee, I have a chemistry exam on Tuesday, November 24, at the same time as the 10am group meeting. Could I be excused from that meeting? I will send my weekly update by Monday, November 23, at 5pm, read the notes afterward, and send any questions by Wednesday, November 25, at noon. Thank you, Maya Brown”

Worked scenario

Why the stronger version works

Because the conflict was known, Maya asks in advance, names the meeting and the date, gives the reason, offers her update early and says what happens next. Had she woken up sick that morning instead, she would not ask permission. She would tell Dr. Lee as soon as she could that she cannot attend, and say when her update and her reading of the notes will follow.

Module 3

Remember

  • Early, prepared, on time.
  • Known conflict: ask. Emergency: notify.
  • Done, next, blocked, hours, questions.
  • Action items: who, what, by when.

Try it this week

Put it into practice

At your next meeting, arrive five minutes early, write down every action item with who and by when, and send your weekly report before the deadline.
